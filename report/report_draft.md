## 1. Problem and track relevance

Domain search engines differ from web search in that they can exploit **document structure**:
zones (title, abstract), metadata (year, category) and **authority** (citations). T6 asks for exactly
this. For scientific literature, authority is usually citation count, and the IR lecture's recipe is
a static quality score g(d) added to relevance, `net-score(q,d) = g(d) + cos(q,d)` [1].

The catch is that citation counts **accumulate with time**. A 2022 paper with 15 citations may be the
exact answer while a loosely related 2012 paper has 4,000; blending raw counts into the score pushes
old papers up regardless of relevance. Beel & Gipp showed that Google Scholar's ranking weights
citation counts heavily, favouring older articles [5, 6]; bibliometrics normalises citations by field
and year for the same reason [7]. On the other hand, for "survey of X" or "seminal work on Y" requests,
authority is exactly what the user wants. Scholar-IR addresses both needs:

1. **Age-fair authority:** g(d) is the percentile of a paper's citations *among papers first submitted
   in the same year* (a cohort percentile), so a strong 2022 paper and a strong 2012 paper score alike.
2. **Query-adaptive λ:** the weight of authority in the net score is predicted per query from cheap,
   inspectable IR features (intent cue words, mean idf, score flatness), high for foundational
   requests and low for recent/specific ones.

An LLM layer sits on top (never replacing the engine): it translates plain English into the query
language, writes an answer from the top-5 abstracts, and every sentence is verified by an
IR-based citation checker (tf-idf cosine + idf-weighted coverage), following the concern that
generative answers must be attributable to sources [8, 9].

**Corpus.** 80000 arXiv papers in cs.IR/CL/LG/AI/CV/DB/SI,
first submitted 2010–2023 (the Hugging Face mirror of the Cornell arXiv dump is a late-2023 snapshot),
sampled per year in one streaming pass so that every year is represented:

*(not generated yet: `results/data/papers_per_year.csv`)*

Citations come from OpenAlex [10]. Matching arXiv papers to OpenAlex is harder than it looks: OpenAlex
merges preprints into their published versions (so arXiv-DOI lookup misses many famous papers),
and our probes found mis-merged records (the landing-page lookup for BERT returns an unrelated
paper with 45k citations; the arXiv DOI of the RAG paper carries a wrong title). We therefore match in
three passes — arXiv DOI, arXiv landing page, title search — and title-check every non-DOI match.

**Pipeline.** request → (optional LLM translation → validated JSON → compiled query) → query parser →
Boolean/phrase/NEAR evaluation ∩ parametric filters → term-at-a-time scoring over postings (BM25F) →
heap top-200 → net score `(1-λ(q))·R + λ(q)·g(d)` → heap top-K → (optional) cited answer → citation checker.

## 3. Beyond IR

* **BM25 and BM25F** (Robertson et al. [2, 3]): BM25F combines zone term frequencies *before*
  saturation, `tf' = Σ_z w_z·tf_z / (1-b_z+b_z·dl_z/avgdl_z)`, which is the principled version of
  weighted zone scoring. Our BM25 agrees exactly with `rank_bm25` once the same idf is used (unit test).
* **PageRank** [4] on the in-corpus citation graph (power iteration, dangling mass redistributed),
  then cohort-normalised like the citation counts (system S8).
* **LLM layer** (Gemini, temperature 0, cached): a pydantic-validated JSON query parser with retry
  and rule-based fallback; an optional LLM intent label as an extra λ feature; cited answer
  generation with a fixed system prompt. The LLM never ranks and never sees more than the top-5
  retrieved abstracts.
* **Citation checker:** `support(s) = max_p α·cos(tfidf(s), tfidf(p)) + (1-α)·idf-coverage(s, p)`, reusing
  the engine's analyzer and idf; plus hard flags for missing citations, fabricated IDs (not among the
  retrieved papers) and numbers absent from the cited abstract.

## 4. Novelty and creativity

* **The age-bias finding, measured without any judgments:** as λ grows, raw citations (A0) pull the
  median publication year of the top-10 back in time and drop the share of recent papers; cohort
  percentiles (A2) do not.

* **Cohort-percentile authority** removes the age effect by construction while still preferring the
  most cited papers *of their year*.
* **Query-adaptive λ** from inspectable features, fitted on dev queries only:
  λ(q) = λ_min + (λ_max − λ_min)·σ(w·x(q)), x = [foundational cue, recency cue, mean-idf z, length z,
  score flatness, optional LLM intent].
* **An IR-native citation verifier** for RAG answers with measured failure modes (§5.4).

Compared with existing tools: Google Scholar and Semantic Scholar expose citation counts and sort
by them, but do not adapt the weight of authority to the request or normalise it by age; our system
makes both explicit and shows the score breakdown (`--explain`).

## 6. Limitations and next steps

* **Corpus snapshot ends in 2023**, and is a stratified 80k sample: most individual landmark papers
  are not in it, so "foundational" queries are judged on what the sample contains.
* **Citation coverage** is incomplete (*(not generated yet: `results/index/authority_info.json`)* is the
  reference year; see coverage table). Unmatched papers receive the cohort median, which neither
  boosts nor buries them; OpenAlex metadata errors (mis-merges, duplicate records that split
  citations) remain.
* **Small, self-judged test set** (10 queries per type on test); pooled recall; unjudged documents
  may surface for configurations outside the pool.
* **Intent features are shallow** — queries without cue words rely on idf/flatness, which separate
  the two types only partially.
* **The citation checker is lexical**: correct paraphrases with little word overlap are flagged.
* **LLM dependency**: answers need an API key; the parser falls back to rules without one.

**Roadmap (course project).** Full arXiv snapshot (2024–26) and all citation data; learning-to-rank
over BM25F + authority + recency features; field-normalised (category × year) cohorts; a dense
retriever fused by reciprocal rank; an NLI-based checker alongside the lexical one; the "papers this
abstract should cite" task using `referenced_works` as ground truth.
